# How Nalu emails sound

Nalu emails should read like a note from a friendly neighbor who knows the roads. Warm, a little playful, never corny, and always quick to get to the point. Code: `src/lib/email/layout.ts`. Sign-in emails: `supabase/email-templates/build.py`.

## Tone
- **Talk like a person.** Write "we" and "you". Use contractions. Say it the way you'd say it over the fence: "Better to know now than at the cones."
- **Warm, then useful.** Open with one human line, then get to the point fast. Every email should leave the rider with one clear thing to do.
- **A little playful, never cute.** One light touch per email is plenty ("No typing addresses half-awake"). Puns, exclamation marks and jokes about traffic misery get old quickly.
- **Plain English for every age.** Short sentences, everyday words. No "leverage", "optimize", "seamless", "journey". If a grandparent and a UH freshman would both get it, it's right.
- **Calm, not alarming.** Roadwork and big event days are heads-ups, not warnings. No ALL CAPS, no "URGENT".

## Hawaiian words
- Use them lightly and correctly: *aloha*, *mahalo*, and *a hui hou* ("until we meet again") are fine. One or two per email at most.
- Always write **Oʻahu** with the ʻokina (ʻ, not an apostrophe). Keep kahakō in place names when we have them (Waikīkī).
- No heavy pidgin, no "brah", no tiki, hula or lei clichés, no surf-bro talk. We're locals helping locals, not a tourist brochure.

## Length and shape
- Marketing emails: about 120 to 170 words. Sign-in emails: a headline and one line.
- One headline, one short intro, a list only when there are real steps or items, one button.
- Sign off from **The Nalu team** with a short, friendly line ("See you on the road,"). A P.S. is welcome when it's kind and true, like the core answer staying free.
- Body text 16px or larger. Every email needs a plain-text version that reads well by itself.

## Subject lines and preheaders
- Under about 50 characters when we can. Say what's inside: "Your week ahead: 2 heads-ups for your route".
- Warm beats clever, and clear beats both. A light "Aloha!" is fine. No clickbait, no fake "Re:", no all caps, no emoji strings.
- Always write a preheader. It finishes the subject's thought, never repeats it.

## Never
- Never invent numbers, minutes saved, stats, rider counts, quotes or testimonials.
- Never promise features Nalu doesn't have, or say Nalu "predicts" or "guarantees" anything.
- Never guess a rider's name (we don't have it, and we never pull one from an email address). No `{name}`, no "Dear rider".
- Never knock Google Maps or Apple Maps. Nalu decides; they navigate.
- Never state event facts (closures, times) we haven't been given from an official source. Link to the official page instead.
- Never guilt or nag people about turning things on, and never hide the unsubscribe link.
